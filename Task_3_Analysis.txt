The comparative analysis of the filter architectures reveals a fundamental trade-off between computational efficiency and phase integrity. While the IIR Butterworth/Chebyshev prototype achieves an aggressive roll-off with a significantly lower order, its inherent phase nonlinearity introduces a varying group delay that is detrimental to Wideband FM. Since the FM demodulation process is based on the time-derivative of the phase, any nonlinear phase curvature manifests as group delay distortion, or "phase smearing," which degrades the transient clarity of the audio. Conversely, the Window-based FIR filter maintains an ideal Linear Phase response, ensuring that all spectral components within the 100 kHz station bandwidth are delayed equally. By preserving this temporal consistency, the FIR architecture prevents harmonic artifacts and ensures that the final "refined, high-SNR" output remains a high-fidelity reconstruction of the original broadcast, making it the superior choice for precision SDR applications.
