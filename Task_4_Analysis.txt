The application of the Wiener Filter resulted in a profound improvement in audio fidelity, specifically through the aggressive suppression of the wideband noise floor—the characteristic "hiss"—that plagued the initial demodulated signal. While the Task 3 filters isolated the station from adjacent channel interference, the Wiener filter functioned as an optimal spectral estimator, dynamically attenuating frequency bins where the noise power dominated. Subjective listening tests confirm that the background static is virtually eliminated during pauses in speech, significantly increasing the Perceived Signal-to-Noise Ratio without introducing significant "musical noise" or artifacts. This transition from a raw, noisy broadcast to a refined, studio-quality output demonstrates the effectiveness of utilizing statistical signal characteristics to achieve optimal denoising in real-world SDR applications.
